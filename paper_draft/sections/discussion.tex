\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\para{Where the predictor is wrong is where the interesting alignment lives.} Our central finding is a useful inversion. A small \gbt\ trained only on base-side features predicts per-token \klt\ well (\secref{sec:results}, $R^2 \approx 0.60$--$0.68$), so most of the base$\leftrightarrow$instruct shift is anticipatable from how uncertain the base model already was. The payoff is not the fit but the miss. When we read the residual---actual minus predicted KL---on the disjoint \justeval\ corpus, the tokens that surprise the predictor are not random and not generic. They are refusal-recovery pivots (the instruct model emitting ``If you have any other questions\ldots'' where the base model ranks ``If'' only third, KL $\approx 19.9$), persona and identity injections (``Alibaba,'' Qwen's maker), knowledge-cutoff framing (``October''), and explicit safety vocabulary (``violence,'' ``politics,'' ``guidelines,'' ``Instead,'' ``recommend''). We surfaced these with no safety labels, no persona annotations, and no discourse parser. The residual itself does the sorting. This is the practical content of H2 and H3: the unexplained part of alignment is structured, and that structure is alignment-specific rather than stylistic.

\para{A quantitative refinement of the Superficial Alignment Hypothesis.} \citet{zhou2023lima} argued that alignment mostly teaches format and style already latent in the base model. Our results let us be precise about what ``mostly'' means and where it breaks. The bulk of \klt\ is explained by base uncertainty---\texttt{base\_tok\_logprob} and \texttt{base\_entropy} carry nearly all of the predictor's importance (\tabref{tab:featimp}), while every stylistic and lexical flag sits below $0.03$. So the part of the distribution shift that is genuinely superficial is exactly the part a base-side predictor can already see: where the base model was unsure, instruction tuning resolves the choice in a stylistically predictable direction. This is consistent with \citet{yang2026branching}, whose branching-factor view holds that alignment sharpens an already-uncertain distribution rather than rewriting it. But the residual is the non-superficial remainder, and it is not negligible---it concentrates on safety, identity, and discourse control. This is the regime \citet{revisiting2024superficial} point to as counter-evidence: behaviors that base-side context cannot reconstruct. We do not have to choose between the two camps. The predictable shift is superficial; the residual is where alignment writes something new, and our method separates them mechanically.

\para{Safety integrates more smoothly as models grow.} The safety residual is positive and significant at every scale, but its magnitude declines monotonically: rank-biserial $0.32 \to 0.30 \to 0.14$ from \qwensmall\ to \qwenmid\ to \qwenbig\ (\tabref{tab:safety_residual}), with mean residual on safety tokens dropping from $+0.138$ to $+0.061$ while regular tokens stay near zero. The Mann--Whitney separation remains overwhelming throughout ($p$ from $\approx 0$ to $2\times10^{-164}$), so the effect is real at $7$B---it is simply smaller. We read this as larger models integrating safety more smoothly into their base-conditioned predictions: the $7$B base already places more probability mass on the cautious continuation, so flipping to a refusal or a hedge is less of a discontinuity and leaves a smaller surprise for the predictor. Safety in a bigger model is less of a bolt-on. The same trend appears in the per-string examples, where the $7$B's high-residual tokens (``Safety,'' ``guidelines,'' ``recommend,'' ``Instead'') are more explicitly policy-shaped than the $0.5$B's bluntly topical ones (``sex,'' ``violence,'' ``politics''), suggesting the small model still relies on coarse keyword triggers.

\para{Limitations.} We are scoring the instruct model's own greedy decode, which is in-distribution for the instruct model and out-of-distribution for the base model---a confound \citet{lin2023urial} flag for URIAL-style comparisons. We mitigate it two ways: we teacher-force the \emph{identical} chat-formatted sequence through both models, so the only thing that differs is weights rather than context, and our analysis lives in the residual, which already factors out the base model's own confidence on that sequence. A token that is merely unusual for the base model is well predicted by \texttt{base\_tok\_logprob} and therefore contributes little residual. That feature is informative by construction---the emitted token is the instruct model's choice---but it is legitimate base-side structure, not leakage from the target: it is computed entirely from the base model's distribution. Our token-class flags are regex-and-vocabulary proxies, not a true part-of-speech tagger, so the class-level statistics (Kruskal--Wallis $p<10^{-22}$ throughout) should be read as coarse groupings. All three pairs are Qwen2.5 instruct models trained with combined RLHF/DPO \citep{ouyang2022instructgpt,rafailov2023dpo}; the gated SFT-only checkpoints we wanted were not downloadable, so we cannot separate SFT-driven from preference-optimization-driven divergence. Finally, we study greedy decoding capped at $80$ new tokens. Longer or sampled generations could shift the position curve and surface late-sequence phenomena we do not see, and a single family at three sizes is a scale \emph{trend}, not a scaling law.

\para{The residual as an unsupervised alignment probe.} Because the residual locates refusal, persona, and safety tokens without any labels, it is a candidate detector rather than only an analysis. The same teacher-forcing-plus-residual recipe extends directly to the logit-difference view of alignment used in proxy-tuning and Emulated Fine-Tuning, where the behavior delta is $\log\Pinst - \log\Pbase$ \citep{liu2024proxy,mitchell2023eft}: our predictor models the expected magnitude of that delta from base-side context, so its residual flags positions where the delta is larger than the base model's own uncertainty would explain. Practically, this gives an interpretability tool that highlights where an instruct model has been steered---which token in a generation is a refusal pivot, an identity assertion, or a safety hedge---and an auditing signal for asking whether a deployed model's safety behavior is smoothly integrated (small residual, as in the $7$B) or brittle and keyword-triggered (large residual, as in the $0.5$B). We see reading the residual as a general move: train a cheap predictor for the predictable part of a model difference, then study what it cannot explain.
